# Comfort & accessibility

Abalassembly offers settings that **genuinely** change the game's display and pace. None of them claims to treat anything: they're choices, offered to anyone who needs them as much as anyone who simply prefers them.

They're named for what they **do**, never after a diagnosis. There's no "ADHD mode" or "gifted mode": putting a clinical label on a button would presume who you are. Calm helps someone in a noisy environment just as much as a mind that's overloaded.

## Where to find them

☰ menu → **Settings** → **Comfort & accessibility** section.

## The settings

| Setting | What it changes |
|---|---|
| **Reduced motion** | Cuts animations and transitions, down to the intro screen and the clock's pulsing. Turns on automatically if your system already asks for less animation. |
| **Plain mode** | Removes playful effects — floating XP, streaks, ornaments — keeping only the game and the information. The content stays, only the decoration disappears. |
| **Stronger focus** | A sharp, constant outline on the active element, for keyboard and touch alike. |
| **Keyboard board navigation** | The board can be played entirely from the keyboard: arrow keys to move, Page Up/Page Down for the two remaining directions (6 in total, matching the sides of a hex cell), Enter or Space to select/play. Every cell has a screen-reader description (contents + coordinate). |
| **Higher contrast** | Darker backgrounds, lighter text, sharper borders, within the game's own palette. |
| **One action at a time** | Dims secondary buttons so only one stands out per screen. |
| **Text size** | Enlarges all text up to 140%. |
| **Spacing** | Loosens line height and letter spacing. |

## What's guaranteed

- **Persistence**: your settings survive a reload.
- **Respecting the system**: on first launch, if your device asks for less animation, calm mode is already on.
- **Reversibility**: one button resets everything to default.
- **Honesty**: an automated test in the regression suite fails if the panel ever starts promising a therapeutic effect. The safeguard lives in the code, not just in the intent.
